Grammar, Not Algebra

Five separate tickets reported that binaries are where this language's precision goes to die. They were all reporting the same bug, and it was never about binaries.

The decision

beam-sharp inherits this platform's type grammar. It cannot inherit its algebra — in any domain.

Erlang already ships <<_:M, _:_*N>> and reasons over it correctly. What it does not ship is an algebra a pessimistic checker can use: every join over-approximates, because Dialyzer is a success-typing tool and may only ever be optimistic. Measured in two independent domains.

One cause, five sightings

The ticket had accumulated five independent reports that binaries are where precision dies, from four different tickets and one piece of literature. Each was recorded as its own problem. Traced to the operation that produces it, they are one problem, and it lives at union — not, as every sighting assumed, at the subtraction that computes ticket 04's residual.

What the sightings shared was the assumption that subtraction was failing. It was not. t_sup collapses two same-constructor binary types into an arithmetic progression before the residual is ever computed, so the declared type is already infinite and subtraction walks it forever. The same algebra keeps <<_:32>> | integer() exact, because different constructors never merge — which is how you know the lossiness was a representation choice rather than a property of the domain.

It generalises, because it happens twice

One lossy domain is a quirk. The finding is that the same failure appears the moment you look at a second infinite domain. erl_types has no general integer intervals at all — it quantises every range onto a fixed ladder of named Erlang types and widens anything else to the nearest rung:

%% measured, OTP 28.5
t_from_range(5, 20)      → 1..255          %% snapped to byte()
t_from_range(1, 1000)    → 1..1114111      %% snapped to char()
t_from_range(500, 2000)  → 1..1114111      %% the same rung
  (1..1000) - (500..2000) → none()          %% a wrong answer from a sound widening
t_from_range(neg_inf, 1) → integer()       %% the bound is simply gone

A trap for whoever runs this next. Ticket 11 filed the Fib debt here, and running it against erl_types reports exhaustive: true — by accident. neg_inf..1 widened to integer(), so integer() - integer() emptied, and the checker never saw n <= 1 at all. A future session would read that as a pass. 20c records it deliberately.

One line, and everything falls on a side of it

Four rules on this map are the same rule: ticket 09 §4's union discriminability, ticket 11 §2's cap on clause-head patterns, ticket 18 §2's admissible foreign declarations, and now this ticket's refinement tiers. All four ask what one BEAM guard decides in O(1). Read this ticket as one judgement applied five times.

The serialisation gap ticket 16 assumed away

Ticket 16 §4 justified generating the encoder on a measured fact: json:encode/1 fails on tuples at any depth, at runtime, and generation moves that to compile time. It treated binaries as the safe case. They fail identically:

json:encode(<<"hello">>)      → "hello"
json:encode(<<255,254,0,1>>)  → ** invalid_byte, 255      %% runtime crash
json:encode(<<0:9>>)          → ** unsupported_type       %% runtime crash

This cannot be fixed inside the grammar just adopted, and the reason is the whole shape of the answer: byte_size reads the term header, and “is this valid UTF-8” reads the content. O(n), not a guard, not a clause head, not a foreign declaration. So text-versus-bytes is not a type in this grammar — it is a refinement, which is how the second decision arrived forced rather than chosen.

What was decided

  • Adopted

    The full binary grammar, with an exact union

    <<_:M, _:_*N>> as Erlang spells it, but beam-sharp keeps a genuine union node where erl_types collapses one. A fixed size is a closed set and provable; any repeating unit is open and takes ticket 12's catch-all under the rule that already exists.

    Exact negation is never needed: the checker must decide whether a residual is empty and whether it is open, and both are decidable here even though the complement has no representation.

    Careful: subsumption is not indiscriminability. <<_:32>> | <<_:32,_:_*8>> absorbs to the open member and is legal; <<_:_*3>> | <<_:_*5>> overlaps at every multiple of 15 bits with neither containing the other, so no guard separates them and ticket 09 §4 errors at the declaration. A reader applying “overlap is an error” would reject a legal type.
  • Adopted

    The whole grammar is admissible at the boundary

    Ticket 18 §2 asked what a foreign declaration may say about a binary. Anything the grammar can spell — the base is byte_size and the unit is a modulus, both guard BIFs, both O(1) (measured: 1M calls on 8 B and on 8 MiB, 2847 µs and 2108 µs, the larger marginally faster).

    This lands opposite to the expectation the ticket was written with. Binaries need no ValidateAs at the boundary where ticket 26's records do — a map erasure needs key-presence and value tests, and a binary needs two arithmetic comparisons.

  • Adopted

    string is binary refined by valid UTF-8

    A bare binary encodes as base64; a non-byte-aligned bitstring is a compile-time error at the encoder, which is ticket 16's own move for tuples applied to the shape it overlooked. A literal is a string by construction, checked at compile time — without that, every string literal in the language would pay an O(n) validation.

    Rejected: base64 for everything (correct, and unreadable to every other system); a runtime check inside the generated encoder (puts the failure back where 16 §4's whole argument was to remove it); a tagged (:utf8, binary) (changes the term, at the platform's most-passed value).
  • Adopted

    Refinements in two tiers — and users get one

    Guard-decidable predicates are reasoned about, legal in clause heads and at FFI, and user-declarable. O(n) refinements are established once by generated code where a value enters, never reasoned about, and are compiler-known only, with string the sole member. The reason is ticket 11's, at a second site: it refused unbounded work “whose size a foreign sender chooses”, and an arbitrary user predicate could be quadratic or fail to terminate where string's check is linear per byte.

    This answers a question standing in the map's fog — whether a user may add to the prelude's second stratum — with a no, and with a reason rather than by omission.

  • Adopted

    Integer intervals join the algebra

    Ticket 08's rule already credited n > 1 as an interval refinement without checking that the platform supplies one. It does not, so beam-sharp builds it — affordable because finite unions of intervals are closed under union, intersection and complement with a decision procedure, and are nowhere near SMT. Ticket 27 had already fenced the cost story by refusing inference, intersection arrows, bounds and row variables.

    What they buy, stated precisely: the right to partition an integer domain by guards without a catch-all, and -spec precision ticket 13 would otherwise widen. Not Fib.

  • Excluded

    Improper lists — a named limit

    is_list/1 returns true for [1,2|3] and is the only O(1) list guard, so list<T> is not decidable at a boundary if it means a proper list. Ticket 18 §2's use of list<term> was optimistic.

    It is not a hole in the guarantee. Run one through the recursion ticket 17 adopted and you get function_clause — ticket 12's retained failure arm doing its job. Outcome 1 or 2, never outcome 3. So the limit is named, the device ticket 18 used for sys:replace_state/2: the crash is deferred from the boundary to the traversal, and never silent.

  • Refused

    Value provenance (taint)

    Per ticket 21: opinionated languages control who may be on the other side, not what the data is. A taint system is a large surface for one hazard that ticket 10 §4's prelude omission already blunts — minting an atom from a runtime-built string has no prelude spelling, so reaching the hazard means reaching for the FFI, where ticket 18's boundary already applies.

Two corrections

Ticket 11 overstated its own debt, in both halves. It said Fib is “only exhaustive and only terminating” with intervals. Exhaustiveness never needed them — the second clause is unguarded and matches everything, so the residual empties regardless. Termination is a promise this language never made: ticket 11's guarantee is coverage, and ticket 12 made partiality the design.

Refinements do not settle ticket 09's newtype gap. This ticket hoped three questions were converging on one mechanism. They are not: a refinement is a set, and naming is pure aliasing, so Meters and Feet as float where value >= 0 remain one type — “non-negative” is not what distinguishes metres from feet. Ticket 09's tuple tag stands unchallenged.
