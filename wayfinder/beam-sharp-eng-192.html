Who Writes The Tag

A record has to erase to something, and every candidate needs a way to tell two same-shaped records apart. That is a tag — and the whole decision turned out to rest not on which erasure carries one, but on whether the compiler or the author puts the value in it.

The decision

A record erases to a map, and record mints a tag from the type’s qualified name — but everything stays structural.

The name enters the term, as data, never the type algebra. The test that proves it is not nominality: a hand-written type carrying the same tag is the same type. So ticket 09’s reasoning survives whole — union closure, negation, the boundary and exhaustiveness all intact — and only its inventory is amended: “there is no record keyword to design” becomes “there is one, and it is sugar for a minted discriminator field”.

What forced it. The ticket was heading for “the author writes a discriminating field by hand” until David named the requirement that fails: in DDD it is very important that Order and Invoice are not the same type — Update(Order o) called with an Invoice must be an error. Under ticket 09 as written that call compiles, and Order | Invoice is not even a union of two things. A hand-written tag fixes it, but it is omittable — and an omitted tag unifies two aggregates in silence, which is exactly the plausible-looking structural drift the standing constraint names as agent authorship’s characteristic failure.

The correction this brief exists to make

My first framing of this question said a tagged map was “nominality in the data, the same objection as the tuple tag”. That was too strong, and it is worth recording that ticket 09 made the identical over-reach — it first wrote “dispatch cannot key on a name”, and amended it on 2026-08-12 after David raised Elixir’s structs. The amendment reads: Elixir puts the name in the term as an atom and dispatches on it, which is §5’s tag remedy at ecosystem scale.

That amendment is now verified at the level of the type representation rather than inferred from behaviour. Reading Module.Types.Descr directly — following ticket 10’s precedent of measuring Elixir’s checker rather than citing it — an Elixir struct type is an open map with the tag as an atom singleton:

%% Module.Types.Descr, Elixir 1.19.5 -- the type of a struct-shaped map
%{map: {:open, %{id: %{bitmap: 4},
                 __struct__: %{atom: {:union, %{Order => []}}}}}}

%% two struct types, same fields, different tags
Descr.empty?(Descr.intersection(order_t, invoice_t))  => true   %% disjoint
%% the same two with the tag removed
Descr.equal?(order_no_tag, invoice_no_tag)            => true   %% identical

So there is no nominal construct anywhere in it. Elixir’s struct “nominality” is structural discrimination on a singleton atom field, and the singleton atom is something beam-sharp already has — ticket 10 made :ok a singleton type and the atom universe open. A tagged map needs no new machinery in this language at all.

What the erasure costs, measured

Ticket 18 measured the tuple discriminator at +13 bytes and asserted — without measuring — that a map’s would be “strictly larger”. It is, but the shape is friendlier than assumed, and the tagged map lands within one byte of the tuple.

Two mechanisms explain the surprises. The exact-set check does not emit a test per field — the BEAM has a single has_map_fields instruction carrying the whole key list, so the +1 B/field is operand growth, not work. And map_get/2 on an absent key fails a guard silently rather than raising (measured, with a runtime-built key so the compiler could not fold it), which is why one test is a complete discriminator for a tagged map: no is_map, no map_size, no per-field presence check.

What Elixir actually enforces

Elixir is the largest shipped instance of “erase a record to a map with a name in it”, which makes its field-set discipline evidence rather than analogy. Measured on 1.19.5, it is weaker than its reputation, and in a direction that matters here.

The finding that cuts against beam-sharp’s current position. Elixir types a struct as an open map and does not enforce exactness on the term at all. Ticket 27 §7 declined row polymorphism, which makes a beam-sharp record an exact field set — and exactness is precisely what costs +29 bytes instead of +14, because a map pattern is open (measured) and only map_size can close it.

So the ecosystem-scale precedent went the other way on the one sub-decision that carries the cost. That is not an argument to follow it — Elixir has no exhaustiveness guarantee to protect — but it means exactness is a beam-sharp-specific cost with no shipped precedent, and it should be paid deliberately rather than inherited.

The three jobs __struct__ does, and their fate here

  • no consumer

    Protocol dispatch

    Dead already, and not on this ticket. Ticket 16 refused every ad-hoc polymorphism construct; open extension needs whole-program consolidation, which fights ticket 13’s aggregate granularity and hot code loading. 16 also found the static-closed variant “not dead, but empty” — consolidation by construction is only available over a closed union, at which point there is nothing left to consolidate.

    So: a beam-sharp tag would carry no dispatch. It is the job that justifies __struct__’s existence in Elixir, and beam-sharp has already declined it.

  • live, and cheap

    Discrimination inside a union

    Ticket 09 requires union members to be discriminable by a synthesised BEAM guard, checked at the declaration. Under a plain map that means value inspection (+44 B measured); under a tagged map it is one map_get (+14 B). This is the job the tag genuinely does here, and it is discrimination, not dispatch — worth keeping the words apart.

  • settled here

    Identity — that an Order is not an Invoice

    This was ticket 09’s named newtype gap, and it had been passed in a circle: 09 said tags cover it and pointed at refinement types; ticket 20 came back and handed it straight back — “a refinement is a set, so Meters and Feet are still one type and 09’s tuple tag stands”. No ticket had ever written down how an opt-in tag is spelled.

    This ticket writes it down. record is the blessed spelling, minting from the qualified name — Elixir’s defstruct being the precedent that it need not be hand-written ceremony in every literal. The gap is closed for records; whether the same form serves Meters/Feet over a scalar is not settled here, and 09 §5’s tuple tag still stands for that case.

The surface, settled

record Order { Id: string, Status: Status, Lines: list<Line>, Paid: Money }

// desugars to, and is interchangeable with:
type Order = { Kind: :order, Id: string, Status: Status, Lines: list<Line>, Paid: Money };

Order o    = Order { Id = "A-1", Status = :draft, Lines = [], Paid = 0 };
Order paid = o with { Status = :paid, Paid = 500 };

o.Status                // lowercase receiver  -> projection
List.Map(f, xs)         // PascalCase receiver -> module qualification

{ ...o, Extra = true }  // refused: spread widens, and 27 s7 closed that
record P { Notes?: string } // refused: k optional fields = 2^k shapes to guard
  • kept

    with alone — and it pays ticket 27’s debt

    27 §7 declined row polymorphism on the assumption that record update would be compiler-visible, width-preserving syntax, and asked to be told if that changed. It did not. Spread is refused because its defining capability is the one thing the type system closed: { ...o, Extra = true } widens, producing a term that carries a minted :order tag while not being an Order — and typing “an Order plus whatever else” is precisely the row variable 27 declined.

    And a non-widening spread is with with a second spelling — the redundancy ticket 17 refused when it killed else.

  • kept

    Construction names the type; the separator is =

    The tag has to enter the term somewhere, so something must name the type at the construction site. Target-typed construction was refused on read cost: mandatory signatures mean most positions carry an expected type, which is what makes the exception nasty — it would work almost everywhere, and the diagnostic would land at the use rather than the literal.

    A correction made during the session: a three-way agreement on : across declaration, pattern and construction was proposed and is wrong. C# deliberately splits colon-for-matching from equals-for-assigning, prototype 01b already wrote it that way, and the atom sigil forces it independently — Status: :placed puts two colons adjacent where Status = :placed reads clean.

  • kept

    The dot projects, disambiguated lexically

    Ticket 17’s refusal does not reach this, for a mechanical reason: xs.Filter(f) needs type-directed resolution of an unqualified name against a candidate set, where o.Total resolves against one declared field set with nothing to dispatch on. Casing settles it before types exist — a type-directed rule would drag back exactly what 17 refused.

    Union projection is legal where every member carries the field, and §1 paid for it without knowing: under a map, map_get is one instruction whichever member arrived; under the tuple erasure it would have needed real dispatch.

  • refused

    Absent fields, and the ? modifier

    The exponential decides it: k optional fields denote 2k shapes, and ticket 18 emits a discriminator on every exported function taking a record. Two spellings for absence would also collide — Notes?: option<string> has three states, two meaning the same thing. TypeScript ships that hazard and grew exactOptionalPropertyTypes for it.

    The modelling consequence is the point, not the consolation: an optional field is usually two record types wearing one name, and §1 just made that cheap to fix. Refusing ? pushes toward illegal states being unrepresentable.

What this leaves owed

Three items, all recorded on the ticket rather than left to rot. The spelling of the minting form — record X { } versus a modifier on type — is a grammar-opinion question and lands in ticket 22’s territory. Ticket 16 §4’s generated encoder must pick between "notes": null and omitting the key for an option<T> holding :nothing, since the type model no longer distinguishes them. And the map’s module-and-namespace fog gains a correctness requirement it did not have: the tag must mint from the qualified name, or Shop.Orders.Order and Billing.Invoices.Order both produce :order and two bounded contexts unify in silence.

One limit worth stating plainly, because DDD usually wants more. The language guarantees shape, never provenance. Tagged, Update(Order) cannot be called with an Invoice. What no BEAM language can guarantee is that an Order-shaped term came from your constructor — ticket 09 §5 and ticket 21 settled it, and prototype 26b re-verified it: a hand-built map compares == to a real Elixir struct. Construction-time invariants stay with ticket 15’s result<T, E> and ticket 18’s boundary guards, which say it about a call rather than about a value’s history.
